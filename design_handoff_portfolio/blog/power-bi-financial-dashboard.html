How to Build a Power BI Dashboard for Financial Data

A practical walk-through of designing, modelling, and shipping a Power BI dashboard that finance teams will actually use — written from dashboards I've delivered for real clients.

Start with the Question, Not the Data

The single biggest mistake I see in finance dashboards is starting with the columns and figuring out the questions later. You end up with thirty pages, eighty visuals, and one finance director who still asks you for the same Excel export every month.

Before I open Power BI Desktop, I get the client to commit to five questions the dashboard must answer at a glance. Just five. Everything else can be drill-down.

The Data Model

Finance data lives in three shapes, almost always: a transactions fact table, a chart-of-accounts dimension, and a calendar dimension. Get those three right and the rest of the model falls out naturally.

A common trap: people put the calendar inside the fact table as a date column and stop there. Don't. A separate calendar dimension lets you write fiscal-year DAX (DATESYTD, SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR) without contorting your filters, and lets you mark fiscal periods, holidays, and quarter cuts as proper attributes.

A Calendar Table I Reuse Everywhere

Calendar = ADDCOLUMNS(
  CALENDAR(DATE(2018,1,1), TODAY()),
  "Year",        YEAR([Date]),
  "Quarter",     "Q" & QUARTER([Date]),
  "Month",       FORMAT([Date], "MMM"),
  "MonthNum",    MONTH([Date]),
  "FiscalYear",  IF(MONTH([Date]) >= 7,
                    YEAR([Date]) + 1, YEAR([Date])),
  "FiscalQtr",   "FQ" &
                    QUOTIENT(MOD(MONTH([Date])-7,12),3)+1
)

DAX That Punches Above Its Weight

Most finance dashboards need the same handful of measures. Get these right early and you'll reuse them across every visual.

  • Revenue. Plain sum of transactions, filtered to revenue accounts.
  • Revenue YoY. CALCULATE([Revenue], SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR(Calendar[Date]))
  • Revenue YoY % change. DIVIDE([Revenue] - [Revenue YoY], [Revenue YoY])
  • YTD Revenue. TOTALYTD([Revenue], Calendar[Date])
  • YTD Last Year. Same as above, wrapped in SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR.
Performance Tip

Materialize anything that gets called more than once per page. A measure that calls another measure that calls another measure can quietly run the underlying aggregation a dozen times. Power Query pre-aggregation is your friend.

Layout: Five Pages, One Filter Set

Every finance dashboard I ship follows the same five-page skeleton:

  1. Overview. One screen, five KPIs, no scrolling. This is the page the CFO opens on their phone.
  2. Revenue. Trend, breakdowns by product line, top customers.
  3. Expenses. Same shape as revenue, mirrored. Trend, breakdowns, top vendors.
  4. Cash & Working Capital. AR aging, AP aging, cash position.
  5. Detail. A flat transaction table for the people who want to verify the numbers.

Every page respects the same three global filters: fiscal period, business unit, and currency. Slicers go in the same place on every page. That's it. That's the rule. Inconsistent slicer placement is the #1 reason finance teams give up on a dashboard.

Closing Thought

A finance dashboard is a product. Treat it like one — name the user, define the five questions, ship the smallest version, and iterate based on what people actually click. The best dashboards I've built were the ones I deleted half of in week two.

Frequently Asked

What is the best data model for a financial Power BI dashboard? +

A star schema with three core tables: transactions fact, chart-of-accounts dimension, and a separate calendar dimension. The calendar enables DATESYTD, SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR, and fiscal-year calculations without filter contortion. Never embed dates only as a column in the fact table.

How many pages should a financial Power BI dashboard have? +

Five pages: Overview (5 KPIs, no scrolling), Revenue (trends + breakdowns), Expenses (mirrored to Revenue), Cash & Working Capital (AR/AP aging), and Detail (flat transaction table). Same three global filters — fiscal period, business unit, currency — in the same position on every page.

What DAX measures are essential for a financial dashboard? +

The five essentials: Revenue (SUM filtered to revenue accounts), Revenue YoY (CALCULATE with SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR), Revenue YoY % (DIVIDE of the variance), YTD Revenue (TOTALYTD), and YTD Last Year. Materialize any measure called more than once per page.
